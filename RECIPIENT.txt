TinyTranscript: audio you put in a Google Drive folder is transcribed by MacWhisper on this Mac,
with speakers named, and saved in Downloads/recordings/processed.

You need: a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or newer), MacWhisper, and a Google account.
Optional: Claude Code (claude.com/claude-code), for titles and for reading free-form file names.

Setup (about 5 minutes, once):

1. Drag TinyTranscript.app into your Applications folder. Keep google-client.json next to it in this
   folder (or put it in Downloads) until setup is done.

2. Open TinyTranscript from Applications. macOS says it cannot check it: open System Settings >
   Privacy & Security, scroll down, click "Open Anyway" next to TinyTranscript, and confirm.

3. A Terminal window opens with the setup. It asks:
   - your name as MacWhisper shows it in transcripts (so your own name is not in file names);
   - to sign in to Google in the browser: choose your account. Google may say the app is not
     verified: click "Continue" (it is a private app);
   then it checks MacWhisper's settings. Fix anything marked with a cross in MacWhisper >
   Settings > Watch Folders: watch Downloads/recordings, subfolder depth 0, export .txt with
   speaker names. Allow Terminal to access Downloads if asked.

4. TinyTranscript appears in the menu bar (a speech bubble). When macOS asks whether TinyTranscript may
   access your Downloads folder, click Allow.

Every day: put recordings (from your phone or computer) into the Google Drive folder
"Recordings inbox". Within a minute they move to this Mac, MacWhisper transcribes them, and the
finished transcript appears in Downloads/recordings/processed, named
<date>_-_<title>_with_<other people>. In MacWhisper, name the voices you know (People): later
recordings then get those names.

The menu bar shows what is happening; "Open logs" shows details if something is red.
